## Ingredients
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 lb Italian sausage (mild or spicy), casings removed and crumbled
- 2 shallots (about 1 cup), finely diced
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano (optional)
- 1-2 teaspoons Calabrian chili paste
- 4-6 garlic cloves, minced
- 6 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1/3 cup vodka
- 4 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 lb rigatoni
- 1/2-3/4 cup heavy cream
- 1-2 tablespoons butter
- 1 cup freshly grated parmesan, plus more for serving
- 1 cup frozen peas (optional)

## Instructions
1. Heat 1 tablespoon olive oil in a large, deep pan or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown 1 lb crumbled sausage, then remove it to a plate, leaving the fat in the pan.
2. Lower the heat to medium. Add 2 diced shallots with a pinch of salt and pepper, 1 teaspoon oregano, and 1-2 teaspoons Calabrian chili paste. Cook until soft, 3-4 minutes.
3. Add 4-6 minced garlic cloves and cook 1 minute. Stir in 6 tablespoons tomato paste and cook, stirring, until it darkens and caramelizes, 2-3 minutes.
4. Deglaze with 1/3 cup vodka, scraping up the browned bits. Add 4 1/2 cups chicken broth and a pinch of salt.
5. Bring to a boil, stir in 1 lb rigatoni, cover, and simmer, stirring occasionally, until the pasta is tender, about 15 minutes.
6. Stir in 1/2-3/4 cup heavy cream, 1-2 tablespoons butter, and 1 cup parmesan. Cook, stirring, until creamy and thickened.
7. Stir the sausage back in along with 1 cup frozen peas and heat through. Adjust salt, pepper, and chili to taste, and serve with more parmesan.

## Notes
- From a TikTok by Here's Your Bite (posted 2025-05-08, "One Pan Meals" playlist).
- The original uses DeLallo shellbow pasta, which is hard to find; rigatoni works well. Check doneness at about 15 minutes, since covered simmering in broth can take a minute or two longer than the box time. If the pot runs dry before the pasta is done, add a splash of broth or water.
- Use more heavy cream for a creamier sauce. The original used a full tablespoon of Calabrian chili paste.
- Yellow onion can stand in for the shallots.
